Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around **8 February 2027**, depending on the moon sighting, with Eid al-Fitr expected around 9 or 10 March. That might feel far away in December, but for a brand in Kuwait or anywhere in the GCC, it's closer than it looks, and a Ramadan social media plan works best when it starts now. Shoots need booking, gift boxes need photographing, and ads need approval before the rush.

This guide gives you a practical **Ramadan social media plan**: what to prepare and when, what to post in each part of the month, and how to keep your content respectful and on-brand. It's written for food, hospitality, perfume, fashion and gifting brands, but most of it works for any business.

## Why Ramadan needs its own plan

Ramadan changes how people live, and so it changes how they use social media.

- **Daily routines shift.** Working hours are often shorter, and evenings become the busiest time of day.
- **Screen time moves later.** Activity often peaks after iftar and late into the night, after taraweeh.
- **Buying habits change.** Families plan iftar and suhoor, host ghabgas, buy gifts and prepare for Eid.
- **Tone matters more.** Audiences expect warmth, generosity and respect, not loud sales.

A plan that just carries on with your normal calendar will miss all of this.

## The plan, phase by phase

### Phase 1: December to early January: decide and book

- **Decide your Ramadan offer.** A menu, a gift box, a special collection, a service. Write down prices and dates now.
- **Book your photoshoot.** Shoot Ramadan products before the end of January, so you have photos ready to design with. Our guide on [preparing products for a shoot](/blog/prepare-products-for-photoshoot.html) has a full checklist.
- **Plan your look.** Decide on colours, props and mood. Lanterns, crescents, dates, Arabic coffee and warm lighting are common, so think about what makes *your* version different.
- **Agree on your budget** for content and ads.

### Phase 2: Mid to late January: create

- **Design your content in advance:** feed posts, stories, highlight covers and reels for the whole month.
- **Write captions in Arabic and English,** each written for its own reader. See our guide to [bilingual captions](/blog/bilingual-captions-arabic-english.html).
- **Prepare your ad creative** and submit ads for review early, as review can take longer during busy seasons.
- **Update your profile:** a Ramadan highlight cover, an updated bio with Ramadan hours, and working links.

### Phase 3: The week before Ramadan: announce

- Share a **"coming soon"** post or story for your Ramadan offer.
- Post your **Ramadan working hours**.
- Publish a warm **"Ramadan Kareem"** greeting on the first day, without a sales message.

### Phase 4: Weeks 1 and 2 of Ramadan: inspire

The first half of the month is about routine and gathering.

- **Iftar and suhoor ideas:** menus, recipes, table settings.
- **Ghabga moments:** sharing platters, hosting tips, group orders.
- **Behind the scenes:** your team preparing for the evening rush.
- **Gentle value:** tips that help people plan their month.

### Phase 5: Mid-Ramadan: Girgian and National Day

- **Girgian:** children's gift bags, sweets and traditional outfits are popular around the 15th of Ramadan in Kuwait and the Gulf. If you sell anything that fits, plan for it early. Get written consent from parents before showing children in any photo or video.
- **National Day and Liberation Day:** respectful content in national colours, with no sales message on the day itself.

### Phase 6: The last ten nights: reflect and give

- Keep content **calm and meaningful**: charity, gratitude, family.
- Many brands slow down sales messages during these nights. Consider doing the same, or keeping promotions gentle.
- Prepare people for **Eid shopping and gifting**, with order deadlines if you deliver.

### Phase 7: Eid al-Fitr: celebrate

- Post an **"Eid Mubarak"** greeting, again without a hard sales message.
- Share **Eid gifts, outfits, sweets and hampers**, with clear delivery cut-off dates.
- Post your **Eid working hours**.
- After Eid, thank your customers and look back at what worked.

## When to post during Ramadan

Your own insights are the best guide, but as a starting point:

- **Before iftar:** a short window for last-minute orders and reminders.
- **After iftar:** a strong time for engagement, often the busiest period.
- **Late evening, after taraweeh:** often a peak time for scrolling, shopping and browsing.
- **Before suhoor:** useful for food and delivery brands.

Keep posting regularly, but don't feel you need more posts than usual. Quality and timing matter more than volume. See our guide on [how often to post on Instagram](/blog/instagram-posting-frequency.html).

## Tone and content rules

- **Be warm and generous,** not pushy. Ramadan is about community, family and giving.
- **Keep humour gentle,** and avoid anything that could seem disrespectful.
- **Don't show eating or drinking in daytime scenes.** Set food content at iftar or suhoor.
- **Choose music carefully.** Many brands use calm sounds or no music during Ramadan.
- **Use modest styling** for models and lifestyle shots.
- **Be honest with offers.** No fake countdowns or offers that aren't real.
- **If you work with influencers,** check that they hold the licence or permit required in your market. Kuwait's new media law requires commercial influencers to be licensed.

## A simple content mix for the month

For a brand posting around 20 feed posts during Ramadan:

- 6 posts about your Ramadan products or menu
- 4 posts with ideas and value (recipes, hosting, gifting)
- 3 posts showing your people and behind the scenes
- 3 greeting posts (Ramadan Kareem, National Day, Eid Mubarak)
- 2 offer posts
- 2 posts for Girgian or the last ten nights

Add daily stories and three to five reels, and you have a full, balanced month.
