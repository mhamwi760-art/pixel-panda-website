"How often should we post?" is the question we hear most from brands in Kuwait. The honest answer is that there's no magic number. Instagram doesn't reward a specific count. What it does reward, and what your followers notice, is content they want to see, shared often enough that they don't forget you. Getting your Instagram posting frequency right is about finding that balance.

Still, "it depends" isn't much help when you are planning next month. So here is a practical guide to **Instagram posting frequency** for small and growing businesses, split by feed posts, stories and reels, with a simple way to pick the right level for you.

## The most important rule: consistency beats volume

Posting 30 times in one month and then disappearing for three weeks does more harm than posting 10 times every month. When an account goes quiet, followers stop expecting to see it, and the next post has to work harder to get noticed.

So start from a number you can keep up **every month**, not the number you could manage in a good week. It is much easier to grow from a steady 10 than to recover from a burst of 30 followed by silence.

## A sensible range for feed posts

For most small and growing brands, these monthly ranges work well:

| Stage | Feed posts a month | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| Starting out, or account is quiet | 8 to 12 | 2 to 3 a week |
| Growing, with steady followers | 16 to 20 | 4 to 5 a week |
| Established, or posting daily offers | 25 to 30 | About one a day |

If you are just starting, **10 posts a month** is a strong base. It's enough to fill your grid quickly, show new visitors that you're active, and test what your audience likes, without burning out your team or your budget.

## Stories: little and often

Stories disappear after 24 hours, so they work best as a steady daily habit. They're where you show the day-to-day: new stock, today's menu, behind the scenes, polls, questions and reminders.

- **Starting out:** a few stories several times a week, around 15 a month.
- **Growing:** stories most days, around 25 a month.
- **Established:** daily stories, 35 or more a month.

Stories also keep you at the front of the row at the top of your followers' feeds, which is valuable in a market as active on Instagram as the Gulf.

## Reels: fewer, better

Reels are where most new people discover you, because they're shown to people who don't follow you yet. But a reel that's rushed rarely travels far. For most brands:

- **2 to 3 good reels a month** is a solid start.
- **4 to 5 a month** if you have the products, people and ideas to film.
- More only if you can keep the quality up.

A short reel with a strong first second will do more than a long one with a slow start. If you need ideas, see our guide to [reel hooks for food and perfume brands](/blog/reel-hooks-food-perfume-brands.html).

## How to choose your number

Ask yourself four questions:

1. **How much new is there to show?** A bakery with a new item every week has more to post than a perfume brand with five products. Match your frequency to real news, not filler.
2. **How quickly can you approve content?** If approvals take a week, a daily plan will fall apart. Be honest about your team's time.
3. **What's the goal this month?** A launch, a season like White Friday or Ramadan, or a new branch may justify a busier month.
4. **What's the budget?** More content needs more design, photography and planning time. Pick a level that you can afford every month.

## What a good month looks like

Here's an example month for a new café posting 10 feed posts:

- 3 posts showing signature drinks and food (product)
- 2 posts about the space and atmosphere (place)
- 2 posts with tips or behind the scenes (value)
- 1 post introducing the team or the story (people)
- 1 offer or event post (promotion)
- 1 post sharing a customer moment, with permission (proof)

Add around 15 stories across the month and 2 reels, and the account looks alive, varied and on-brand.

## Timing in Kuwait and the GCC

Your own Instagram insights will tell you best when your followers are online, so check them after a few weeks. As a starting point, many Gulf audiences are most active around lunchtime and in the evening, after work and dinner. During Ramadan, activity often shifts later, after iftar and taraweeh.

Pick regular slots and stick to them, so your followers learn when to expect you.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Posting just to fill the calendar.** A weak post can do more harm than no post.
- **Using the same photo again and again.** Fresh photos make a big difference.
- **Forgetting captions.** A good caption explains, invites and gives a clear next step.
- **Only posting offers.** If every post is a sale, people tune out. Mix in value, people and proof.
- **Ignoring Arabic.** In the Gulf, bilingual captions reach more of your audience. See our guide to [bilingual captions](/blog/bilingual-captions-arabic-english.html).
