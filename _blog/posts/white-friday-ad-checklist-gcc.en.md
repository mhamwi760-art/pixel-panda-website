**White Friday ads** are the busiest, most expensive ads of the year in the Gulf. In 2026, Black Friday falls on 27 November, and across Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the rest of the GCC most retailers stretch it into a full week, or even the whole of November, under the "White Friday" name. Feeds fill up with red and yellow "SALE" banners, and every brand is paying for the same eyes at the same time.

That means a weak ad costs more than usual, and a rejected ad can cost you the whole weekend. This checklist walks through everything we check before a White Friday campaign goes live. Use it with your team, your designer or your agency.

## 1. Lock in the offer first

The creative can only be as clear as the offer behind it. Before anything is designed, write down:

- **What exactly is the offer?** "20% off all perfumes" is clear. "Huge savings" is not.
- **Which products are included,** and which are not.
- **The real start and end dates and times,** in Kuwait time (GMT+3) or the local time of each market.
- **Any limits:** stock, one per customer, online only, selected branches.
- **The price before and after,** if you will show both. The "before" price must be one you actually charged.

Be honest here. Kuwait's consumer and digital commerce rules expect ads to show real prices and real offers, and Meta, TikTok, Snapchat and Google all reject or restrict ads with offers that aren't available, hidden conditions or fake countdowns. A fake timer might look exciting, but it can get your ad pulled and damage trust.

## 2. Pick one message per ad

In a crowded feed, people decide in about a second. Each ad should carry one idea:

- One product, or one clearly defined collection.
- One offer.
- One action: Shop now, Order on WhatsApp, Visit the store.

If you have three offers, make three ads. It also helps you learn which one works.

## 3. Design for the first second

The best White Friday creative is not the loudest. It is the clearest.

- **Show the product big and early.** If it's a reel, the product should be on screen in the first second, not after a long intro.
- **Keep text on the image short.** A headline of a few words and the offer. Put the details in the caption or ad text.
- **Stay inside the safe zones.** Profile names, buttons and captions cover the top and bottom of stories and reels. Keep the offer and logo away from the edges.
- **Use your brand colours, not just "sale red".** When every ad is red and yellow, a feed that looks like *your* brand stands out.
- **Make it readable without sound.** Most people scroll with sound off, so add captions or on-screen text to video.

## 4. Make every size you need

Each placement has its own shape. Prepare them before launch, not on the day.

| Placement | Shape | Common size |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram and Facebook feed | 4:5 portrait | 1080 × 1350 |
| Stories and reels (Instagram, Facebook) | 9:16 vertical | 1080 × 1920 |
| TikTok | 9:16 vertical | 1080 × 1920 |
| Snapchat | 9:16 vertical | 1080 × 1920 |
| Square fallback | 1:1 | 1080 × 1080 |

Check each platform's current specs before you export, as they change from time to time.

## 5. Write the copy in Arabic and English

In the Gulf, bilingual ads are not a nice-to-have. Many of your customers will scroll past an English-only ad, and many others will scroll past Arabic-only.

- **Write each language on its own,** don't translate word for word. "Limited time" and "لفترة محدودة" do the same job, but a literal translation of a joke or a pun rarely does.
- **Use the words people actually use.** "خصم" and "عرض" are clearer than formal phrases. Gulf customers often mix English words into Arabic, like "سيل" or "أونلاين", and that's fine if it sounds natural.
- **Check numbers and dates in both versions.** It's easy to update the English price and forget the Arabic one.
- **Keep Arabic text right-aligned** and in an Arabic font that is built for it, not a Latin font.

## 6. Check the platform rules for your category

Different categories have different rules. A few that catch GCC brands out every year:

- **Before-and-after images** for weight loss, skin or cosmetic results are restricted or banned on most platforms.
- **Beauty, health and supplements** may need age targeting (18+) in some markets.
- **Counterfeit or "inspired by" products,** like perfume dupes, can get ads and accounts banned.
- **Fake buttons, fake play icons and gimmicky text** like "CLICK HERE!!!" are rejected.
- **Music:** use licensed tracks or each platform's commercial music library for ads.
- **AI-made or heavily AI-edited images** may be labelled automatically. Don't try to hide or remove those labels.

If you work with influencers for White Friday, remember that Kuwait's new media law requires commercial influencers to hold a licence, and Saudi Arabia and the UAE have their own permit systems. Ask for the creator's licence or permit number before you sign.

## 7. Test the landing page

Most failed campaigns fail after the click. Before launch:

- Open the link **on a phone**, on mobile data, not office Wi-Fi.
- Make sure the page shows the **same offer and price** as the ad.
- Check that the page loads quickly and isn't covered in pop-ups.
- If the ad says "Order on WhatsApp", test the WhatsApp link and make sure someone will answer during the sale.
- Check the Arabic version of the page too.

## 8. Plan the timing

- **Submit ads for review early.** Review can take longer during busy weeks. Aim to have everything approved a few days before the sale starts.
- **Warm up your audience.** A teaser post or story a few days before tells followers to expect something.
- **Schedule a "last day" ad,** but only if it really is the last day.
- **Have a plan for when stock runs out,** so you are not paying for ads on a sold-out product.

## 9. Decide what you will measure

Agree on the numbers before launch, so you can judge the campaign fairly afterwards:

- Reach and cost per result.
- Clicks or WhatsApp chats started.
- Sales or orders, if you can track them.
- Which creative and which language performed best.

Write down one lesson for next time, whatever happens. Ramadan is less than three months away, and the same lessons apply.

## The checklist, in one place

- Offer written down: products, dates, limits, real before and after prices
- One message per ad
- Product visible in the first second
- Short text on image, inside the safe zones
- All sizes ready: 4:5, 9:16, 1:1
- Arabic and English copy written separately and checked
- Category rules checked, no fake timers or buttons
- Influencer licences or permits collected, if any
- Landing page tested on a phone, in both languages
- Ads submitted early, teaser planned
- Results to measure agreed
