Photography

How to prepare your products for a professional photoshoot

By The Panda Paw · 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Good product photography in Kuwait starts long before anyone picks up a camera. The photographer brings the lights and the eye, but the brand brings the products, and the state those products arrive in decides how much of the shoot goes into making great pictures and how much goes into fixing small problems. A dented box, a smudged bottle or a missing lid can cost twenty minutes each.

This guide is the checklist we wish every brand had before shoot day. It works whether you sell perfume, pastries, abayas, skincare or home décor, and whether you are shooting with us or anyone else in the GCC.

Step 1: Decide what the photos are for

Before you gather a single product, write down where the photos will be used. It changes everything about the shoot.

  • Instagram feed posts usually need a portrait 4:5 crop, with space for a headline if you plan to add text.
  • Stories and reels covers are tall 9:16, so the product should sit in the centre with room above and below.
  • Your website or online store often needs clean shots on a plain background, all at the same angle.
  • Ads need the product to read clearly at a glance, even on a small phone screen.
  • Delivery apps have their own image rules, so check them before the shoot.

Share this list with your photographer. A good shoot plan covers each use, so you are not stuck cropping a landscape photo into a story later.

Step 2: Choose your hero products

You will rarely have time to shoot everything well. Pick the products that matter most this season:

  • Your best sellers.
  • Anything new, or launching in the next two months.
  • Seasonal items, for example Ramadan boxes, National Day editions or winter collections.
  • Products that look most different from each other, so the feed doesn't feel repetitive.

Then put them in order of priority. If time runs short, the top of the list is already done.

Step 3: Make the products camera-ready

Cameras see far more than eyes do. Under studio light, small marks become big ones.

For packaged products

  • Bring at least two perfect units of each product, so there is a backup if one gets marked.
  • Check every box and label for dents, scuffs, bent corners and stickers that aren't straight.
  • Remove price tags, shop stickers that won't be on the final product, and any protective film (unless the film is part of the look).
  • Wipe glass and shiny surfaces with a microfibre cloth. Fingerprints show on perfume bottles more than anywhere else.

For food

  • Cook or bake on the day, as close to the shoot time as possible.
  • Make more than you need. The first one is rarely the best-looking one.
  • Bring the sauces, garnishes and toppings separately, so they go on fresh in front of the camera.
  • Tell the photographer about anything that melts, sinks or dries out quickly, so those shots go first.

For fashion and accessories

  • Steam or iron everything the night before and transport it on hangers.
  • Bring clips and pins to adjust the fit on a model.
  • Check jewellery and watches for dust and scratches.

Step 4: Gather props and backgrounds that match your brand

Props should support the product, not compete with it. A simple rule: everything in the frame should belong to your brand's world.

  • Use your brand colours for backgrounds, cloths or small accents.
  • Add one or two textures that tell a story: marble for a luxury perfume, a wooden board for bakery items, linen for modest fashion.
  • Bring ingredients or materials that explain the product, such as coffee beans, oud chips, flowers or fabric swatches.
  • Avoid props with other brands' logos on them.

If you are not sure what fits, a moodboard helps. It is a single page of reference images that shows the mood, colours and styling you want. We prepare one before every shoot, and it saves a lot of back and forth on the day.

Step 5: Write a short shot list

A shot list is just a table of the photos you need. It does not need to be fancy.

Add any must-have angles, such as the back of the label or the inside of a bag, and any shots you have seen and loved from other brands, as inspiration rather than copies.

Step 6: Plan for models, hands and people

If people will appear in your photos:

  • Decide early if you need a full model or just hands. Hands are often enough for food, skincare and jewellery.
  • Agree on clothing colours that suit the product and the brand.
  • Get written consent from anyone who appears, and a guardian's written consent for anyone under 18. Under Kuwait's new media rules, consent for minors matters, so treat it as a must.
  • Make sure the styling suits your audience and the local culture.

Step 7: Sort out the logistics

The boring details make or break the day.

  • Confirm the location: a studio, your shop, your kitchen or a hotel space. If it is your space, clear the area and check the natural light at the planned time.
  • Pack products in boxes with soft wrapping so nothing arrives damaged.
  • Bring a cleaning kit: microfibre cloths, cotton buds, a lint roller, scissors, tape and glue dots.
  • Have one person from your team on set who can approve shots and answer questions quickly.
  • Charge your phone. You'll want behind-the-scenes clips for your stories.

Step 8: Agree on what happens after the shoot

Before the day ends, confirm:

  • How many edited images you will receive, and when.
  • Whether you'll get the photos in the sizes you listed in Step 1.
  • How many rounds of changes are included.
  • Who owns the images and where you can use them.

Getting this in writing avoids surprises later.

A quick checklist to save

  • Uses listed: feed, stories, website, ads, apps
  • Hero products chosen and in priority order
  • Two perfect units of each product
  • Labels, boxes and glass cleaned and checked
  • Props and backgrounds in brand colours
  • Moodboard approved
  • Shot list shared
  • Model consent forms signed
  • Location, cleaning kit and on-set contact confirmed
  • Delivery, sizes and usage rights agreed
